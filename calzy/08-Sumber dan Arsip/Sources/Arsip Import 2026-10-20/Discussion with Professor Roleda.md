## Summary

### Environmental Warning System Project Presentation

Team Devu from Apple Developer Academy in Jakarta presented their final project focused on developing environmental warning systems related to CIPP (Coral bleaching, Ice Syndrome, and Pollution Pollution) using Pinu Island in Kapalawan City as their study case. The team, led by Calzy, explained how they initially considered helping fishermen catch fish but expanded their focus to seaweed aquaculture after discovering Indonesia's potential in this area. Salwa shared that her initial interest stemmed from enjoying fish, which sparked the team's broader exploration into marine environments and seaweed cultivation.

### Seaweed Cultivation Monitoring Solution

Calzy presented a solution to help community advocates in seaweed cultivation recognize environmental conditions associated with ice ice, an issue affecting farmers on Tidu Island. The proposed solution involves an iOS app combining IoT sensors and satellite data processed through machine learning to provide early warnings and visualizations of potentially stressful conditions. Key uncertainties discussed included whether temperature and salinity parameters are sufficient for the solution and what specific threshold values should be used for salinity and temperature. MiRo mentioned his experience supervising engineering students working on similar monitoring projects for seaweed disease recognition.

### Water Quality IoT Sensor Deployment

MiRo and Raditya discussed the deployment of IoT sensors for monitoring water quality, focusing on measuring parameters like salinity, temperature, and light to develop an early warning system for ice-ice disease. MiRo suggested using conductivity measurements instead of direct salinity readings to gather additional information about water quality and potential contaminants. The discussion emphasized starting with basic parameters and collecting time-series data to establish patterns and correlations.

### Seaweed Ice Disease Research

MiRo explained that ice ice disease in seaweed progresses through a series of events starting with plant stress from high temperature and light, which affects photosynthesis and pigment content before leading to disease. MiRo emphasized the need for ground monitoring of environmental factors including water quality, microbes, and other stressors that weaken plant immunity. Calzy mentioned their research identified four main contributors to seaweed ice ice, including temperature, salinity, water currents, and UV light, though they haven't found specific numerical thresholds for creating a model.

### Seaweed Salinity Tolerance Research

MiRo explained that while typical salinity recommendations suggest 28-32 as optimal, his student's research found that eukaryotic seaweeds can tolerate lower salinity levels (down to 24-25) when compensated by higher nutrient content, particularly nitrate and phosphate. MiRo noted that this physiological principle applies to temperature as well, with higher nutrients potentially compensating for negative temperature effects in other seaweed species. MiRo offered to send the relevant research paper to Calzy for further literature review on salinity thresholds.

### Seaweed Farming Temperature Requirements

MiRo discussed optimal sea surface temperature ranges for seaweed farming, explaining that while temperatures between 26-30 degrees Celsius are ideal, levels up to 32 degrees can be tolerated if other environmental factors like nutrients and water circulation remain good. He emphasized the importance of collecting long-term data series combined with biological observations to understand how environmental factors affect seaweed over time, noting that negative effects from exposure like low salinity might not be immediately observable but could appear hours later. MiRo also addressed the challenge of relying solely on seaweed farmer reports, cautioning that farmers sometimes exaggerate or provide inconsistent accounts of sudden issues like the ice-ice disease, which has actually been observed for decades and varies by seaweed strain rather than being solely a result of climate change.

### Ice-Ice Disease Detection Challenges

MiRo discussed the challenges in early detection of ice-ice disease in seaweed, highlighting the need for improved observation practices by hatchery staff and farmers. He explained that while early warning systems can provide valuable environmental data, they may not directly benefit farmers who prioritize immediate financial returns over long-term crop health. MiRo emphasized the importance of changing farmer behavior through education, as inconsistent monitoring contributes to disease outbreaks. The discussion also covered the distinction between bleaching due to exposure and microbial-related softening, with MiRo agreeing to share relevant research papers with the team.
